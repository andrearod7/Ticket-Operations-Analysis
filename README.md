## Project Overview

This project analyzes an operational ticket management process to evaluate whether the team's processing capacity was sufficient to keep up with incoming ticket demand during the analysis period.

Using a simulated dataset based on a real operational workflow, the analysis focuses on identifying backlog growth, measuring processing performance, and uncovering operational insights through KPI design and dashboard reporting.

The project demonstrates an end-to-end analytics workflow, including data validation, preparation, analysis, and visualization using Microsoft Excel.

## Business Problem

The team needed to determine whether its existing processing capacity was sufficient to keep up with incoming tickets. A large backlog was already suspected, creating a risk of delayed resolutions and potential escalations.

The analysis aimed to quantify the backlog during the available analysis period, compare incoming and completed tickets, and identify operational factors that could contribute to the gap between demand and processing capacity.

## Dataset

* **Records:** 399 tickets
* **Analysis period:** January–March
* **Source:** Simulated dataset based on a real operational workflow
* **Tool:** Microsoft Excel

The dataset includes ticket creation and resolution dates, status, ticket type, priority, team, and calculated resolution time. Additional date-based fields were prepared to support weekly and monthly analysis.

## Methodology

1. **Data Audit** – Validated data types, missing values, ticket status, dates, and category consistency.
2. **Data Preparation** – Prepared the dataset for analysis and created calculated fields for weekly and monthly analysis.
3. **Operational Analysis** – Compared incoming and completed tickets, tracked backlog growth, and analyzed resolution time and ticket priority.
4. **Dashboard Development** – Built an Excel dashboard with KPIs and visualizations to communicate operational performance and support decision-making.

## Key Findings

* **Backlog increased throughout the analysis period:** 399 tickets were received compared with 109 completed, resulting in a final backlog of 290 tickets.
* **Processing capacity remained below incoming demand:** Incoming tickets exceeded completed tickets in most weeks, causing the backlog to grow consistently over the analysis period.
* **Series tickets required more resolution time:** Average resolution time was higher for Series tickets (131.6 hours) than for Movies (117.4 hours), suggesting that Series work may require greater processing capacity.
* **Medium-priority tickets represented the largest open workload:** 170 Medium-priority tickets remained open, compared with 46 High-priority and 70 Low-priority tickets, indicating that the backlog was concentrated primarily in Medium-priority work.
